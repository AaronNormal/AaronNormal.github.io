Meta splits the budget between ads in the same ad set itself, so some ads get almost no spend. A cheap early result on a low-spend ad can mean it deserves more budget, not that it's failing.

What families said is fair

—

Included in the estate charge

  • Estate & amenity fee: 24-hour security and gatehouse, parkland and woodland walks, play areas and sports pitches, roads, paths and lighting, waste and recycling, water and drainage, full family access to the main pool and slides, village events, the planted boundary, shared building insurance, a reserve fund and estate management.
  • Residents' club: a calm residents-only pool for lane swimming, a gym and studio, and a club lounge.
  • Heat network: upkeep of the shared ground-source heat network (regulated by Ofgem), and smart meters with one monthly bill.
  • Not included: the energy you use (billed at local rates, generally cheaper because most is made on the estate), council tax, school fees, and extras such as private lessons and food.

What's counted and what's left out
  • The site only saves people once they finish the quiz, so families who stopped partway aren't counted.
  • Test sign-ups are left out: every one marked as a test, plus 6 of Aaron's and the team's that weren't marked.
  • Names, emails and phone numbers never reach this page. Locations are postcode districts only (like RH12).
  • Cost per family uses spend and families up to the same day, so families who joined today count from tomorrow.
  • Visitor numbers are the Plausible figures from the 9am Slack digest.
